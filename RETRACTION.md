# Retraction: the "rank-one gate" finding

**Withdrawn.** Earlier commits in this branch reported that NSNet2's mask decision has a
rank-one input-frequency × output-frequency coupling — "a global speech-presence gate, not a
per-bin SNR estimator" — with an oracle Wiener positive control said to validate the
measurement. **That result is an artefact of the estimator and does not hold.** It appeared in
`REVIEW.md`, `PLAN.md`, `PAPER.md` and `diagnostics/coupling_matrix.py`; all are corrected.

## What was wrong

The statistic was `R[f_in, f_out] = Σ_t |∂z(t₀,f_out) / ∂L(t,f_in)|` — absolute value taken
**before** summing over time. For a zero-mean Jacobian, `E|A| > 0`, so
`Σ_t |A_t| ≈ T·E|A| + O(√T)`. The mean term is a constant positive matrix — exactly rank one —
carrying `O(T)` energy, while every bit of rank information sits in the `O(√T)` fluctuation.
The estimator manufactures rank one out of anything dense.

Fed synthetic Jacobians of known rank (F=257, n_out=64, T=60), it reports:

| true structure | top-1 SV | participation ratio |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| **full rank (257)** | **0.991** | **1.02** |
| rank 64 | 0.991 | 1.02 |
| rank 8 | 0.991 | 1.02 |
| rank 1 | 0.995 | 1.01 |
| diagonal | 0.027 | 60.95 |

Discrimination between true rank 1 and true rank 257 is **0.004**. The values reported for
NSNet2 (top-1 SV 0.925–0.992, PR 1.02–1.17) lie **inside the full-rank null**. Reproduce with
`diagnostics/estimator_null.py`.

## Why the control did not catch it

The oracle Wiener gain is per-bin by construction, so its off-diagonal entries are *exactly*
zero — `|0| = 0`, and no constant floor forms. Its diagonal enrichment of 15.37 is not a
property of the model at all: it is `1/(area fraction within ±8 bins)` = **15.42**, the
geometric ceiling of the metric. That is why the number looked so reassuringly stable across
`n_out` — it was pinned at its maximum.

The control was sound for what it actually tested (does the instrument detect *locality*?) and
was being asked to certify something else (does the instrument estimate *rank*?). **A positive
control only licenses the property it varies.** A rank control has to vary rank — synthetic
Jacobians of known rank, which is the table above and costs a few seconds.

## What survives

Little. The NSNet2 numbers are indistinguishable from a dense full-rank null. LiSenNet scored
PR 3.25–4.20, which is *above* the null of 1.02 and so is not purely floor — but since the
statistic is not a rank measure, that difference cannot be read as "LiSenNet's decision is
higher rank than NSNet2's". Every cross-architecture claim built on it is withdrawn.

Two things do survive, because they never depended on this statistic:

* The repaired attribution pipeline and its ground-truth validation (`REVIEW.md`), where the
  original pipeline scores at chance (1.04× enrichment) and the fixed one reaches 6.3× against
  a random control at 1.03× and an energy-detector control at 2.25×.
* The probe result: a 400-MAC linear probe on NSNet2's GRU2 state predicts per-frame speech
  over-suppression at AP 0.468 against 0.298 for the best signal needing no model internals,
  held out by speaker *and* noise type on LibriSpeech.

## Replacement direction

A **forward-only** statistic on the model's own predicted gain surface `G[f,t] = σ(z)` avoids
the defect entirely — no Jacobian, no abs-before-sum — and admits real nulls and ceilings:
random gain and phase-shuffled `G` as nulls, the oracle IRM and Wiener gain as ceilings, with
effective rank reported as `r90`/`r99` rather than top-1 SV (a random surface scores top-1 SV
0.75, so that quantity is uninformative here too). Under verification; nothing will be claimed
from it until the nulls are measured through the identical code.

## The lesson, which belongs in any paper that comes out of this

Every structural statistic needs a **matched null generated by the same estimator**: synthetic
data with the property set to known values, pushed through the identical pipeline. A control
that separates the extreme case (exactly diagonal vs everything else) can look like validation
while being blind to the axis actually claimed. This is the second estimator defect found in
this branch — the first was a diagonal-band metric whose width silently scaled with sampling
density — and both were caught by a control, not by inspection.
